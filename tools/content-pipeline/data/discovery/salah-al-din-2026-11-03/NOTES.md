# Salah al-Din governorate discovery — 3 November 2026

This batch adds 29 Salah al-Din records, which brings the governorate from 5 records to 34. Ten of them have map pins.

- Every new record has `status: needs-review`, at least one source, and alternative names.
- `build_salah_al_din_records.py` defines the batch.
- **UNESCO components.** Components of Samarra Archaeological City (UNESCO 2007) and Ashur (UNESCO 2003) link to the approved parent records through `parentSite`. Their `heritage_status` says they are not separately inscribed.
- **Approved records not changed:** Samarra, the Great Mosque and Malwiya, Abu Dulaf, the Caliphal Palace (Dar al-Khilafa / al-Jawsaq) and Ashur.

## Sources

- **UNESCO** property pages for Samarra and Ashur, and the Samarra nomination document (search excerpt).
- **Specialist sources:** Archnet (Qubbat al-Sulaybiyya), Northedge's Samarra survey papers, the Metropolitan Museum (Herzfeld, Ashur) and the French Ministry of Culture.
- **Wikipedia** for the Askari shrine, Qasr al-'Ashiq, Tell es-Sawwan, Kar-Tukulti-Ninurta, Imam Dur, al-Arba'een, the Green Church and the Balad shrine.
- **Wikidata** for coordinates.
- **Details removed.** Before the build, details not found in a fetched source were removed: the second Askari bombing, the link between the racecourse and Tell al-'Alij, and specific dates and positions for several Ashur components.

## Review flags

- **Duplicate avoided.** The C1-01 "al-Mashuq Palace" and "Qasr al-'Ashiq" are the same palace and are now one record. The Caliphal Palace (al-Jawsaq) is already approved, so Wikidata's separate Jawsaq items are not repeated.
- **Thin records.** The 12 Ashur components and Tell al-'Alij say only what the UNESCO pages support. Field publications (Andrae; Northedge's *Archaeological Atlas of Samarra*) would fill them in.
- **Uncertain sources:**
  - The racecourse description rests on a fragmentary excerpt of Northedge's paper.
  - The Tikrit citadel has no detailed archaeological source.
- **Approximate pins:**
  - The Abbasid Samarra pin sits in the northern part of a ruin-field tens of kilometres long.
  - Tell es-Sawwan's coordinates are rounded.
  - The Balad shrine's pin is on the town.
- **Lost heritage:** Imam Dur, al-Arba'een and the Green Church were destroyed in 2014. The Askari shrine was bombed in 2006 and rebuilt.

## Not entered

- **Wikidata-only items:** Tell Umm al-Sakhr, al-Musallakhah and the Tikrit Grand Mosque.
- **Wikidata canals:** al-Rasasi, al-Abbas, Nahrawan and Dujayl. These belong to the Abbasid irrigation landscape but have no descriptive source here.
- **Tuz Khurmatu and al-Shirqat towns:** no heritage-specific source.
