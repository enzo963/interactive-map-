# Anbar governorate discovery — 3 November 2026

Anbar had no records before this batch. It now has 33, and 8 of them have map pins.

- Every record has `status: needs-review`, at least one source, and alternative names in ancient, Arabic and English forms where sources give them.
- `build_anbar_records.py` defines the batch.

The province reports about 438 known sites, mostly along the Euphrates, but its last survey (2002) was never finished and no named inventory is public. Most of what is documented comes from the 1979–83 Haditha (Qadisiya) Dam salvage campaign. The reservoir later flooded much of that valley. As requested, records favour academic and governmental sources over tourism sites:

- ICOMOS Monumentum
- Kokushikan University
- The Polish Centre of Mediterranean Archaeology (via Wikipedia)
- The French Haradum publications
- The British Institute for the Study of Iraq's Ana report
- MDPI Remote Sensing
- The University of Anbar
- Iraq's National Investment Commission
- An Iraqi academic journal on Hit Citadel
- The head of the antiquities authority and the Anbar antiquities inspector (quoted by Al Jazeera)

## Review flags

- **Flooded and lost sites.** Records from the Haditha survey carry a note that the site may now be under or beside Lake Qadisiya. Qal'a Island, 'Usiyeh and Bijan are known to be submerged. Haradum was not reached. The old Shaykh Rajab mosque is half-submerged.
- **Thin descriptions.** The B-section entries of the ICOMOS survey could not be re-read in full, because the PDF text did not extract. Those records give only name, locality and locator, carried over from the C1-01 pass.
- **Dating disputes:**
  - The al-Farouq (al-Ma'mura) minaret: pre-Islamic or Abbasid?
  - The Pumbedita and Nehardea identifications for ancient Anbar.
  - The al-Ma'mura minaret named by Al Jazeera is taken to be the al-Farouq minaret. Confirm this.
- **Rounded coordinates.** Haradum and ancient Anbar have rounded Wikidata coordinates and are marked `approximate`. The norias pin is the town of Hit.
- **Two Tell Aswads.** Tell Aswad near Haditha is a different mound from the Tell Aswad at ancient Anbar.

## Leads not entered

- **Desert "Qasr" names in the Western Desert** (Wikidata only, from gazetteer place names): Qasr al-Khubbaz (Haditha district), Qasr Amij (Rutba), Qasr Thumayl, Qasr al-Bardawil, Qasr al-Halqum. No academic source was found confirming that they are ancient structures. Finster and Schmidt's Iraqi desert survey (*Baghdader Mitteilungen*) is the place to check.
- **Wadi Hauran and the Rutba desert.** Prehistoric sites and rock art are mentioned in general terms only.
- **Tell al-Barud.** A visit was reported in April 2026; the article was not accessible.
- **Khan al-Baghdadi.** The town is named after a caravanserai, but no source describes the khan.
- **Habbaniya War Cemetery** and the Ibn Hashim tomb: Wikidata only.
- **Al-Hashimiyya.** Its location is uncertain.
- **Modern landmarks:** mosques and bridges in Fallujah and Ramadi.
