# Anbar and Diyala discovery notes

- 118 candidate records: 27 Anbar and 91 Diyala. These are discovery candidates, not verified map additions.
- Principal expansion source: Ghassan Taha Yaseen, “Settlements of the Hamrin Basin, Iraq, from Early Times to the End of the Old Babylonian Period”, Adumatu 11 (2005), pp. 27–38. Map 1 on p. 28 names 70 tells. The accessible indexed excerpt exposes the map's numbered inventory; full PDF fetch in the browser failed due to size. Records therefore correctly use search-snippet access, with numbered locators.
- Hamrin entries have regional locality evidence, but modern governorate assignment is inferred. Every map-derived record explicitly flags administrative boundary checking. Do not promote these wholesale without checking coordinates against modern boundaries.
- ICOMOS/Monumentum, “The Ancient Sites in the Basin of the Haditha Dam on the Euphrates”, pp. 85–92, supplied smaller Anbar sites and named landscape components. Full text was accessible.
- University of Chicago ISAC, Kokushikan University and CRAST provide excavation-project corroboration for major Hamrin tells. National Investment Commission provides explicitly provincial Diyala inventory entries and Hit Citadel locality.
- Parent/component records intentionally coexist: Songor versus A/B/C; Kheit Qasim versus I/II/III; Telbis bank/island sites; Al-Khaliliya settlement/mosque/minaret; Jafrin/caverns; Old Ana/Lubbad island/relocated minaret. Do not interpret raw record count as independent settlements.
- Possible identity conflicts to resolve: Khibet al-Qasim and Tell Abu Qasim; map Kumeideh (44), prose Rubeidheh (44), and ar-Rubeideh (69). The last remains separate and is explicitly flagged rather than silently merged.
- Historical reservoir survey evidence does not establish present survival, accessibility, visibility or location above water. No coordinates or visiting claims are supplied.
- Excluded despite some institutional mentions: Sherwana Castle (likely outside modern Diyala in Sulaymaniyah), al-Qusayr churches and Ukhaidir (Karbala). Also excluded unnamed Khanaqin church and uncorroborated cave/monastery names.
- Remaining gaps: Anbar western-desert caves, named monasteries, military/Islamic remains outside the Haditha basin, and mosque/shrine inventories; Diyala Islamic urban fabric, Mandali, Baladruz and Khanaqin religious buildings. Exact original-map inspection, Arabic-name normalization and modern boundary checking are the next priorities.
