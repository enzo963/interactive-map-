# Kurdistan candidate discovery — C1-01

150 records: Erbil 36, Duhok 18, Sulaymaniyah 96. All are `candidate-needs-review`. Research date: 2026-11-02.

Main inventories consulted in full text: Ur et al., EPAS 2021 (Harvard-led, IRAQ; PDF mirror at Kurdipedia); Altaweel et al., Shahrizor Survey Project 2012 (UCL repository); Tsuneki et al. 2015 and Saber 2025 in Kokushikan University's AL-Rafidan PDFs; Columbia University Mapping Mesopotamian Monuments catalogue. Supplementary institutions include UNESCO, Cambridge, Tübingen, Heidelberg, LMU Munich, German Archaeological Institute, Slemani Directorate of Antiquities, Kurdistan Regional Government, Board of Investment/French Consulate, Duhok Archaeological Park, MESOPOTAMIA heritage organization, and the University of Liverpool/Sulaymaniyah team's PLOS ONE article.

Records state search-snippet access where used without opening the source; full-text records were opened with web tools. Source locators preserve survey IDs and table/figure references.

Review priorities:

- The 2012 Shahrizor paper used the then-current Sulaymaniyah province. It includes the plain toward Halabja, now a separate governorate. Every SSP-derived record flags this uncertainty; current provincial assignment is provisional, not approval.
- Raparin/Peshdar records retain Sulaymaniyah with administrative review notes. Turkaka's exact boundary requires confirmation.
- Columbia explicitly assigns Lalish, Jerwan, Khinnis, Gunduk, and Rabban Hormizd to Nineveh. These were excluded from the three-governorate contribution rather than reassigned to Duhok based on KRG management or tourism usage.
- Shakar Tepe was conservatively folded into Gird-i Shakar as a possible alias. Tell Haji and Quchika Tepe still require comparison with the SSP inventory. Hazar Merd's TJ 28 survey record may relate to the cave complex; no extra cave record was counted.
- Published different survey names are retained where equivalence has not been established. Tepe Kal was counted once across two inventories.
- Amadiya's staircase, gate, reliefs, mosque, and former archdiocese are components, with parentSite notes. Rabana and Merquli are adjoining components of one integrated fortification complex. Components must not be treated as additional independent settlements in aggregate site statistics.
- Current survival varies. Highway salvage sites and rebuilt churches are retained as historic sites, with damage/reconstruction qualifications where sources provide them. Descriptive bathhouse name is explicitly marked as descriptive.
- No coordinates were invented; locations remain textual. No modern hotels, dams, or ordinary museums were entered.
