# Ninewa / Salah al-Din / Kirkuk candidate discovery

194 records: Ninewa 148, Salah al-Din 25, Kirkuk 21. All remain candidate-needs-review.

Main sources: Czech Academy of Sciences' destroyed Mosul monument catalogue; Mesopotamia Heritage's Mosul, Nineveh Plain and Kirkuk inventories; UNESCO/SBAH Old Mosul and Kirkuk tentative dossiers; UNESCO Ashur and Samarra dossiers; University of Tokyo UMUT photo database; Heidelberg University's Nineveh excavation site; ORACC; LMU Munich's Nuzi project.

Separate parent complexes and their named buildings/gates/mounds are intentional, with component relationships recorded in notes. Cemetery records are separate where the source explicitly inventories them. Similar church names retain denomination or locality to avoid conflating different buildings.

Current governorate boundaries require independent review for Khazir-side sites El Khan and Mlefaat, and for Ayn Sifni/Mam Chevan. Historical Tokyo database regional labels are not treated as modern governorate authority. Jarmo and Karim Shahir were excluded because the old Kirkuk regional label would mislead current governorate assignment; Lalish and other administratively sensitive border-area shrines were also omitted. The dataset does not assert precise coordinates.

The Samarra ICOMOS PDF is too large for full web-tool fetching, so its four supported monument entries honestly use search-snippet access. Museum PDFs and several ORACC/Penn entries likewise use indexed excerpts where full text was not opened successfully. No commercial travel site or Wikipedia is a sole source. Present condition and authenticity are not approved by inclusion.

This is broad discovery, not an exhaustive provincial register. Small sites absent from reliable fetched inventories were not invented to increase counts.
